**MAP-21: the original interval.**

The historical synthetic setup fixes the counted cycles to one second's defining-reference count and sets the normalized endpoint correction equal to the fractional offset. In normalized coordinates, let `a = 1/1000`, `y = d/s = ±a`, each with probability `1/2`, and

\[
U=T/s=\frac{1}{1+y}+y.
\]

The original native/physical-admission statuses remain unclaimed. This is a declared metrology model, not a derivation of seconds from AOD events. Both denominators are positive.

For the two-dimensional normalized input `(y,d/s)`, its covariance is `a² [[1,1],[1,1]]`, a valid singular positive-semidefinite matrix. At the input mean, the Jacobian is `(-1,1)`, so the linearized variance is zero. Direct two-point algebra instead gives

\[
\mathbb E[U]=\frac{1}{1-a^2}=\frac{1000000}{999999},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(U)=\frac{a^6}{(1-a^2)^2}
=\frac{1}{999998000001000000}>0.
\]

The two output values are the mean plus/minus `a³/(1−a²)`. The mean shift from the nominal value 1 is `1/999999`. Under the declared scale, the variance carries `s²`; its exact standard uncertainty is `1/999999000 s`, approximately `1.000001×10⁻⁹ s`. The unchanged old implementation incorrectly reported complete first-order synthetic uncertainty with variance zero. The current propagator returns the exact mean and variance with `exact_for_declared_joint_law` status. If only the original means and covariance are supplied, it correctly retains a zero diagnostic but leaves nonlinear output moments unresolved.

\[
V=Rf/\mathrm{Hz}=1+\delta x-bx^2,
\quad \mathbb E[V]=1-\frac{b\epsilon^2}{2},
