\subsection{C1 -- Solar perihelion test on a 100-year system window}\label{app:c1}
\paragraph{Question.} Does a cited 100-year solar-system ledger, read as one shared n-soma system with a nested Earth--Moon subledger, carry the Mercury perihelion witness while preserving shell/ring support, dominant residual-duration class $B^*$, torsional witness, cadence support, and effective progress bookkeeping?

The emphasis is the AOD calculation spine itself: once the cited body or cluster is reduced through the loss/tension channel, the orbital closure family should already be visible. The section therefore makes explicit why the present reported class stabilizes at a :6 closure rather than a flatter or more extended outer report.

\paragraph{Cited input block.} The rendered solar-system inputs for this section are fixed by one cited dictionary only. Sun mass is taken from NASA's \emph{Basics of Spaceflight, Chapter 1: The Solar System}.\footnote{NASA, \href{https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter1-1/}{Basics of Spaceflight, Chapter 1: The Solar System}.} Planetary masses and sidereal periods are taken from the JPL Solar System Dynamics planetary physical parameters table.\footnote{JPL Solar System Dynamics, \href{https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html}{Planetary Physical Parameters}.} Heliocentric semi-major axes and eccentricities are taken from JPL's approximate-positions element tables.\footnote{JPL Solar System Dynamics, \href{https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html}{Approximate Positions of the Planets}.} The Moon's mass is taken from the JPL planetary-satellite physical parameters table, and its descriptive semi-major axis, eccentricity, and sidereal period are taken from the JPL planetary-satellite mean-elements tables.\footnote{JPL Solar System Dynamics, \href{https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/}{Planetary Satellite Physical Parameters}; JPL Solar System Dynamics, \href{https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/}{Planetary Satellite Mean Elements}.}

These cited values instantiate this example only. After the reduction below, the row is recorded in the default calculus and the SI layer appears only in the rendering block.

\paragraph{Declared reduction.} Let
\[
\Sigma_{\mathrm{sol},100y} = [\mathrm{sun},\mathrm{mercury},\mathrm{venus},[\mathrm{earth},\mathrm{moon}],\mathrm{mars},\mathrm{jupiter},\mathrm{saturn},\mathrm{uranus},\mathrm{neptune}]
\]
be the cited 100-year solar-system ledger.

Each orbital participant is reduced to an ominion on the cited window. The bracketed sublist $[\mathrm{earth},\mathrm{moon}]$ is retained as a nested satellite subledger inside the larger system rather than flattened into a planet-like row. Mercury perihelion is then read as an extracted slice of the same shared ledger.

\paragraph{Reported class.} For the present orbital/body examples, the reported class is a \texttt{tetron:*:[\dots]:6} class. The reason is not an arbitrary naming preference. After reduction through the loss/tension channel, :6 is taken as the first chirality-supporting outer report: it is the smallest outer enclosure that carries the present body-scale torsional/cadence reading without collapsing back into a lower outer sketch. Higher outer reports such as :8 and :10 remain available as later shell extensions, but they are not the first stabilized class used in this line.

\paragraph{Calculation block.} This section shows the AOD spine in compact form rather than jumping directly from cited inputs to a rendered witness.

\textit{Step 1: body-to-rate anchor.} For each cited body or cluster $i$, attach the optional SI-facing rate anchor
\[
m_{z,i} := \frac{m_i c^2}{h},
\]
using the rendered SI mass $m_i$ only as an input-dictionary anchor. The corresponding quarter-level count is
\[
n_i := \log_3(m_{z,i}), \qquad \lambda_i := \frac{n_i}{4}.
\]
For the present rendering, the Sun and Mercury anchors are
\[
m_{z,\odot} \approx 2.698\times 10^{80}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1},\qquad \lambda_{\odot} \approx 42.144,
\]
\[
m_{z,\mathrm{Mercury}} \approx 4.478\times 10^{73}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1},\qquad \lambda_{\mathrm{Mercury}} \approx 38.591.
\]
These are render-layer anchors only; the row itself is not built from floating-point mass ratios.

\textit{Step 2: loss/tension reduction.} The cited body or cluster is then reduced through the main-note loss channel on the same window:
\[
m_i \longrightarrow m_{z,i} \longrightarrow R_{\mathrm{loss},i},
\]
with
\[
R_{\mathrm{loss},i}(\omega) = \frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{eval}}} \sum_{t\in(\omega\cap O_i)_{\mathrm{act}}} \log_3\!\left(\frac{3}{m_t}\right).
\]
This is the decisive AOD step: once the body is reduced through $R_{\mathrm{loss}}$, the closure family is already constrained by the same loss/tension bookkeeping that governs cadence, torsion, and residual-duration support elsewhere in the note.

\textit{Step 3: system-ledger formation.} Form the shared 100-year ledger on $\Sigma_{\mathrm{sol},100y}$ and retain $[\mathrm{earth},\mathrm{moon}]$ as a nested subledger. The rendered horizon is one hundred Julian years,
\[
T_{\mathrm{eval}} = 100 \times 365.25 = 36525\ \text{bip}
\]
on the daily report grid.

\textit{Step 4: extracted Mercury slice.} Read Mercury perihelion as an extracted orbital slice of the same system ledger, not as a disconnected two-body law:
\[
S_{\mathrm{Mercury}} := [\mathrm{sun},\mathrm{mercury}] \subset \Sigma_{\mathrm{sol},100y}.
\]
Rendered orbit counts on the same 100-year horizon are attached afterward by
\[
N_i^{(100y)} := \frac{100\ \text{Julian years}}{P_i},
\]
so that, for example,
\[
N_{\mathrm{Mercury}}^{(100y)} \approx 415.20,\qquad N_{\mathrm{Earth}}^{(100y)} \approx 100.00,\qquad N_{\mathrm{Moon}}^{(100y)} \approx 1336.85.
\]

\textit{Step 5: closure and burden extraction.} On the extracted Mercury slice, the return-duration burden chain is formed in the standard order
\[
b(\kappa) \longrightarrow H_B \longrightarrow B^* \longrightarrow \Lambda_b,
\]
then the same cited ledger carries
\[
A_B,\ R_{\mathrm{loss}},\ \mathcal{N}(\omega),\ v(\omega),\ T_{\mathrm{tors}}.
\]
For the current row, the leading support class is reported as
\[
B^*=137,\qquad \Lambda_b=6,\qquad R_{\mathrm{loss}} = \frac{1+8\ell_2}{24},\qquad T_{\mathrm{tors}}=3.
\]
\textit{Support note.} The present row does not compute $B^*$ from $R_{\mathrm{loss}}$. On the cited window, $B^*$ is read from the return-duration burden chain
\[
b(\kappa) \longrightarrow H_B \longrightarrow B^* \longrightarrow \Lambda_b,
\]
whereas $R_{\mathrm{loss}}$ is read from the loss/tension channel. Both are carried by the same cited ledger and reported together, but neither replaces the other.

\textit{Step 6: source monon-cycle extraction.} For the present body/orbital line, the source beat is not inserted as a free hypothesis. Instead, the supplement uses a local quarter-cycle causality-speed extractor on the same cited ledger:
\[
\chi_4(O_i;\omega) := \lambda_i - \Lambda_b(\omega) - \Bigl\lfloor 24\,R_{\mathrm{loss}}(\omega)\Bigr\rfloor.
\]
The counting convention is the quarter-level / tesseract sequence
\[
0,1,2,3,10,11,\ldots,
\]
so raw $\lambda_i$ is only the body-scale quarter-level address. The stabilized monon beat is the remaining quarter-count after support mass and loss-service ticks are paid on the same cited ledger. On the present source line,
\[
\chi_{4,\odot}
\approx
42.144 - 6 - \Bigl\lfloor 24\cdot\frac{1+8\ell_2}{24}\Bigr\rfloor
=
42.144 - 6 - 3
=
33.144.
\]
Thus the rendered source monon beat attached to the shared 100-year system ledger is
\[
P^{\mathrm{mon}}_{\odot} \approx 33.14\ \mathrm{y},
\qquad
N^{(100y)}_{\odot} \approx \frac{100}{33.14} \approx 3.02.
\]
This source beat is therefore calculated from the cited loss/support ledger rather than inserted by hand.

\paragraph{Why :6 and not :8 or :10.} The present body/orbital line is carried by the first chirality-supporting outer report. In the main note, the minimal cage-band witness is $(4{:}6)$, so the same algebra already singles out a six-side outer report as the smallest enclosure that can carry the present orbital torsion/cadence reading without reverting to a flatter sketch. Higher outer reports remain available as later shell extensions, but they are not needed to stabilize the present solar-body class.
